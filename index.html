The last-mile problem

Your data centre is rarely the problem. The last stretch of the link is.

Load shedding, cable theft and FWA latency that swings from 20 ms to 200 ms are not fixed by buying more bandwidth. They are fixed by how traffic is balanced, protected and kept alive across the links you already have.

Load shedding

Each load-shedding cycle stresses FWA base stations and LTE handoffs, so network performance drops exactly when it is needed most.

Cable theft

In some regions more than 40% of outages are caused by copper or fibre theft. A single-link site can be down for hours or days.

Unreliable last mile

FWA and LTE are best-effort services. Good enough for streaming, but not for POS systems, ERP or a call centre.

High MPLS costs

Private circuits cost two to three times as much as comparable broadband, and can still fail on the last mile.

Multi-site complexity

Multiple branches, multiple ISPs, multiple firewall brands. Your IT team ends up managing every site individually.

Unseen failures

Packet loss, jitter and dropped sessions are easy to miss until users report them. Per-packet visibility lets you spot problems before your customers do.

The uptime math: 20 components at 99.9% plus one interconnection at 99% gives only 87% end-to-end uptime, roughly 87 days of downtime a year. Bonding two independent chains raises that to 98%.

What people assume, and what is true

✗ "SD-WAN is just smart routing."

It is per-packet session architecture with congestion control and IP continuity. Routing is one component; the whole system is the value.

✗ "You need FEC for clear voice."

Proactive congestion management and smart reordering deliver clear voice without the extra bandwidth that FEC requires.

✗ "You will be locked to one vendor."

The solution works with any ISP (fibre, LTE, FWA, satellite, MPLS) and any firewall (Cisco, Fortinet, Palo Alto).

✗ "It needs expensive hardware."

It runs on commodity hardware, with the focus on resilience and session continuity rather than expensive appliances.

Questions we hear most

Short answers

Will it work with our existing firewalls?

The SD-WAN node sits alongside your existing security stack, not instead of it. It works with Cisco, Fortinet and Palo Alto firewalls.

How long does a multi-site rollout take?

Zero-touch provisioning means no on-site visits. A typical 10-site rollout takes two to three weeks including testing, and 50 sites have been brought online in a single day.

What happens during a full power outage?

UPS keeps the edge node alive during the outage. When power returns, sessions resume automatically, with no reconnection and no dropped calls.

Can we try it on one site first?

That is exactly how we recommend it: a free last-mile assessment, a pilot on one branch, measurement, then scale. No commitment until it has proven its value.
